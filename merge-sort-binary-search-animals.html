How it works

1. Divide: cut the line of animals in the middle into a left group and a right group.

2. Keep dividing: cut every group in half again and again, until each group has just 1 animal. One animal alone is already sorted.

3. Compare: look at the front animal of two neighbouring groups and pick the smaller number.

4. Merge: put the smaller one into a new group first. Keep going until the two groups become one sorted group, then repeat until the whole line is sorted.

Before searching: binary search needs a sorted line. So first we sort the animals with merge sort: cut the line in halves, keep cutting, compare, then join the pieces back in order.

1. Look in the middle: pick the middle animal of the range between lo and hi.

2. Compare: if it matches, you're done. If it's too small, the target must be to the right.

3. Drop half: move lo or hi past mid, so half of the animals are ruled out in one go.

4. Repeat until you find it, or lo passes hi, meaning it isn't there.

The list must be sorted. Each step halves the range, so it takes about log₂(n) steps.
